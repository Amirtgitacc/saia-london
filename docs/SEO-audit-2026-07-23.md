# SAÏA London — technical SEO audit & migration review

**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Scope:** the redesigned site as it actually ships — the live Shopify storefront
(`www.saialondon.com`, theme "SAÏA v2", published 2026-07-22), the `theme/` source in this repo,
the static build it was ported from, and the Vercel/GitHub Pages deployments.
**Method:** repo review plus live inspection — sitemaps, `robots.txt`, served HTML for the home
page, all 11 ported pages, a sample of legacy pages, the blog, collections and products.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The redesign moved the *design* across faithfully and the commerce path works end to end (the
2026-07-22 QA took a real order through checkout with correct maths). What did **not** come
across is the layer that decides whether pages rank: page titles, descriptions, internal links to
older content, and the templates that render everything the redesign didn't explicitly rebuild.

The single biggest problem is the home page. It is the site's money page for "yoga mat hire
London", and it went live with the title `SAÏA London` and a meta description about a "Social
Club empowering women" that never mentions mat hire. The redesign's own title —
`SAÏA London: Yoga Mat Hire Across London` — exists in this repo but never reached the live head,
because the porter strips `<head>` and the Shopify admin SEO fields were never updated.

The second is scale of collateral. The old store had ~40 pages, 28 blog articles, 10 products and
a collection, all still live and still in the sitemap. The new theme routes 11 of those pages.
Everything else fell through to stub or bare-fallback templates: no navigation, no footer, and in
two cases no content at all. The blog — 28 articles including "our yoga mat rental service in uk"
— had **zero** internal links pointing at it from anywhere on the new site.

Third, the same content is being served on three hostnames. `saia-london.vercel.app` and
`amirtgitacc.github.io/saia-london` both returned 200 with no canonical and no `robots.txt`,
competing directly with the live store. The GitHub Pages copy is 69 commits stale and still
quotes the retired £35 courier price.

None of this is fatal and most of it is recoverable. The URLs themselves were preserved — the
page handles match, `www` and HTTPS are enforced correctly, and Shopify's sitemap is intact.
Nothing was deleted; it was orphaned and unlabelled. Both are fixable in code, and most of it is
fixed in this pass.

**A note on timing:** the theme was published on 2026-07-22, one day before this audit. Ranking
and traffic effects will not be visible in Search Console yet. Everything below is diagnosed from
the served HTML and the URL inventory, not from measured traffic loss — a distinction I keep
explicit throughout.

## 4. Confirmed SEO regressions

These are verified against the live site, not inferred.

### R1 — The home page lost its title and description
- **Severity:** Critical
- **Affected:** `www.saialondon.com/` · `theme/layout/theme.liquid`, `tools/shopify-port/port.mjs`
- **Evidence:** live HTML returned `<title>SAÏA London</title>` and
  `<meta name="description" content="Social Club empowering women through fitness, community and
  mindset. Monthly In Real Life Events">`. The static source `index.html:6-7` carries
  `SAÏA London: Yoga Mat Hire Across London` and a mat-hire description. `port.mjs:36-40` strips
  `<title>` and meta on port; `theme.liquid` then fell back to `page_title`/`page_description`,
  which read from admin fields still holding the old site's copy.
- **Why it matters:** the title tag is the strongest on-page ranking signal and the clickable line
  in results. The commercial page for "yoga mat hire london" was competing with a title
  containing no keyword at all, and a description describing a different business.
- **Fix:** new `theme/snippets/seo-meta.liquid` sets title/description per route in code.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes (ships on next theme push)
- **Expected impact:** high. Restores keyword relevance and click-through on the primary page.
- **Risk of fix:** low. Additive; unlisted routes still use the admin values.

### R2 — Two meta descriptions on the home page
- **Severity:** High
- **Affected:** `theme/templates/index.liquid:2`
- **Evidence:** live HTML contained two `<meta name="description">` — the old one in `<head>`, the
  redesign's inside `<body>` (templates render through `content_for_layout`, which is in the body).
- **Why it matters:** a description in the body is not reliably read; the head one won, so the
  redesign's copy never had effect. Conflicting duplicates also invite Google to ignore both and
  synthesise its own snippet.
- **Fix:** removed the body copy; the head now carries it via `seo-meta.liquid`.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** medium · **Risk:** low

### R3 — 28 blog articles orphaned
- **Severity:** High
- **Affected:** `/blogs/news`, `/blogs/resources` (28 articles) · `theme/snippets/blog-chrome-foot.liquid`
- **Evidence:** grep across `theme/**` returned **zero** `href` values containing `/blogs`. The
  live home page likewise contained no link to the blog. The articles remain in
  `sitemap_blogs_1.xml`.
- **Why it matters:** these are the site's only genuine informational content assets — including
  "our yoga mat rental service in uk", "the best yoga retreats in the uk 2023" and a long run of
  Brunch Club write-ups. With no internal links they receive no link equity from the new site and
  are discoverable only via the sitemap; Google generally demotes content it can't reach by
  crawling.
- **Fix:** "Journal" link added to the shared footer and every ported page footer;
  `port.mjs` now injects it automatically so future re-ports keep it.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** high · **Risk:** low (one link, existing styles)

### R4 — ~29 legacy pages render with no header, no footer and no links
- **Severity:** High
- **Affected:** `/pages/about`, `/pages/faq`, `/pages/yoga-mat-rentals`, `/pages/my-story`,
  `/pages/craftsmanship`, `/pages/size-guide`, ~23 more · `theme/templates/page.liquid:14-18`
- **Evidence:** the sitemap lists 40 pages; `page.liquid` routes 11 by handle. The rest hit the
  `{% else %}` branch, which rendered only `<h1>{{ page.title }}</h1>{{ page.content }}`. Live
  fetch of `/pages/yoga-mat-rentals` returned a page with content but no `bnav` header and no
  footer markup.
- **Why it matters:** `/pages/yoga-mat-rentals` is a plausible legacy ranking page for "yoga mat
  rental london". Any visitor landing there from search saw an unstyled page with no way into the
  site, and crawlers found no outbound links to follow. Orphaned pages bleed authority.
- **Fix:** the fallback branch now renders inside `blog-chrome-head`/`blog-chrome-foot`.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** high · **Risk:** low (ported pages untouched)

### R5 — The whole site duplicated on two other hostnames
- **Severity:** High
- **Affected:** `saia-london.vercel.app`, `amirtgitacc.github.io/saia-london` · `vercel.json`
- **Evidence:** both returned HTTP 200 serving `<title>SAÏA London: Yoga Mat Hire Across London</title>`;
  `robots.txt` 404 on both; no canonical tag on either. The `gh-pages` branch is 69 commits
  behind `main`. Task 11 Step 3 of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-16-shopify-full-move.md`
  planned the Vercel redirects; `vercel.json` had no `redirects` key.
- **Why it matters:** three copies of the same content compete with each other, split whatever
  authority they attract, and let Google pick the wrong canonical. The stale GitHub Pages copy
  also publishes the retired £35 courier price, which contradicts the £90/£45 the live store
  charges.
- **Fix:** per-path 301s in `vercel.json`, each to its closest Shopify equivalent, with `/api/*`
  untouched; a `robots.txt` that deliberately **allows** crawling so the redirects are seen.
- **Fixed:** ✅ Vercel yes · ⚠️ GitHub Pages needs Amir to disable it in repo settings
- **Impact:** high · **Risk:** low-medium (see §12 for the one thing to verify after deploy)

### R6 — `/collections/all` lost its content and contradicts its own title
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `theme/templates/collection.liquid`
- **Evidence:** live fetch returned `<title>All Products</title>` with `<h1>Mat hire, made
  simple</h1>`. The template is a fixed stub that ignores the collection entirely.
- **Why it matters:** title/H1 mismatch is a relevance signal conflict, and a URL in the sitemap
  that discards its own content is wasted crawl budget and a dead end for anyone landing there.
- **Fix:** `seo-meta.liquid` gives it a matching title/description; the template gained outbound
  links to the journal, events and contact.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** low-medium · **Risk:** low

### R7 — `/collections` rendered a completely empty page
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `theme/templates/list-collections.liquid`
- **Evidence:** the template was `<main style="…"></main>` — a 200 response with no content, no
  nav, no links. Textbook soft 404.
- **Fix:** now mirrors the collection page. **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** low · **Risk:** low

### R8 — Product pages reduced to a five-line stub
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `theme/templates/product.liquid`, 10 product URLs
- **Evidence:** the template rendered `{{ product.title }} {{ product.price }}` and nothing else.
  `/products/yoga-mat-hire.json` shows the product still holds real copy: mat spec, dimensions
  (68 × 185 cm × 4 mm), natural-rubber base, PU surface, non-toxic and PVC-free.
- **Why it matters:** that spec copy is genuine, relevant content that existed before the redesign
  and was being discarded at render time.
- **Fix:** the template now renders the product description inside the site chrome, with a link to
  the estimator and **no** add-to-cart (mats are hire-only).
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** medium · **Risk:** low

### R9 — `/products/yoga-mat-hire` was deindexed
- **Severity:** Medium
- **Affected:** `theme/layout/theme.liquid:11-14` (before the fix)
- **Evidence:** `{% if request.page_type == 'product' %}<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` —
  a blanket rule. Live fetch of `/products/yoga-mat-hire` confirmed `noindex`.
- **Why it matters:** the rule is right for the eight checkout-plumbing products, but this URL
  predates the redesign and is the most likely legacy ranking page for the site's core term.
  `noindex` discards whatever authority it holds.
- **Fix:** replaced with an explicit handle allowlist; the hire product is indexable again, and
  `noindex` now carries `, follow` so link equity still flows out of the plumbing pages.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** medium · **Risk:** low — the cart adds lines by variant ID
  (`theme/config/settings_data.json:4-9`), never by handle, so indexability can't affect checkout.

### R10 — `story.html` description truncated in results
- **Severity:** Low
- **Evidence:** 203 characters; Google truncates around 155-160.
- **Fix:** rewritten to 161 characters, keeping the founder hook and the "a woman who wins"
  meaning; also set in `seo-meta.liquid` so it overrides the long version in admin.
- **Fixed:** ✅ yes · **Impact:** low · **Risk:** none

## 5. Potential SEO risks

Real risks, but not confirmed as regressions — either they predate the redesign or they need data
I don't have access to.

### P1 — Thin and duplicate legacy pages still indexable
- **Severity:** Medium · **Fixed:** ❌ needs Shopify admin
- **Evidence:** the pages sitemap includes `/pages/copy-of-shipping`,
  `/pages/copy-of-yoga-mat-rental`, `/pages/product-details`, `-1`, `-2`,
  `/pages/sca-affiliate-empty-page`, `/pages/shipping-planners`.
- **Why it matters:** obvious duplicates and empty placeholders dilute site quality signals. Names
  like "copy-of-" and "empty-page" suggest they were never meant to be public.
- **Recommended fix:** in admin, redirect each to its closest live equivalent — `copy-of-shipping`
  and `shipping-planners` → `/pages/shipping`; `copy-of-yoga-mat-rental` → `/pages/yoga-mat-rentals`;
  the `product-details` set → `/products/yoga-mat-hire`; `sca-affiliate-empty-page` → unpublish.
  Check Search Console for impressions first — if one has traffic, keep it and improve it instead.

### P2 — Founder content split across three URLs
- **Severity:** Medium · **Fixed:** ❌ needs a content decision
- **Evidence:** `/pages/story` (new), `/pages/my-story` (legacy, title "Our Story") and
  `/pages/about` (legacy, title "Meet The Founder Video") all cover Cristina and the brand origin.
- **Why it matters:** classic keyword cannibalisation — three pages competing for the same query,
  each weaker than one consolidated page would be.
- **Recommended fix:** pick `/pages/story` as canonical, fold anything worth keeping from the
  other two into it, then 301 both. Do this **after** checking Search Console: if `/pages/about`
  holds the impressions, redirect the other way.

### P3 — Mobile flow copy exists only in JavaScript
- **Severity:** Medium · **Fixed:** ❌ deliberately not touched
- **Evidence:** `js/home-mobile-journey.js:28-35` holds seven chapters of copy as JS strings,
  injected via `root.innerHTML`. It is not in the initial HTML.
- **Why it matters:** Google indexes mobile-first. This copy carries the real hire proposition —
  "£8.50 each", "minimum of ten", "free pickup from our NW3 warehouse". Google does render JS, but
  rendering is deferred and not guaranteed; content only in JS is weaker than content in HTML.
- **Mitigating factor:** the desktop `.flow-rail` bands carry equivalent copy in the HTML, so the
  facts are not invisible — but the mobile rendering path is the one Google indexes from.
- **Recommended fix:** move the seven chapters into the HTML and have the JS read them from the
  DOM rather than write them. This is a real refactor of an animation the client explicitly loves,
  so it needs its own task and testing — not a change to slip into an SEO pass.

### P4 — Sitemap lists URLs that are not indexable
- **Severity:** Low · **Fixed:** partially
- **Evidence:** `sitemap_products_1.xml` lists all 10 products, eight of which are `noindex`.
  Shopify generates the sitemap and does not respect theme-level robots meta.
- **Why it matters:** cosmetic rather than harmful — Search Console will report "Excluded by
  noindex" for those URLs. Worth knowing so the report isn't mistaken for a real problem.
- **Note:** narrowing the noindex (R9) already cut this from 10 to 8.

### P5 — Media weight on the home page
- **Severity:** Medium · **Fixed:** partially
- **Evidence:** `assets/saia-yoga-person-volumetric.glb` is 9.7 MB; `assets/flow.mp4` is 28 MB
  locally and **404s on the Shopify CDN** (files over 10 MB are skipped by `port.mjs:110`).
  `theme/assets/` totals 90 MB.
- **Why it matters:** heavy media is the main lever on LCP for a scroll-driven page like this one.
  The `flow.mp4` 404 also suggests a render path may be silently failing on the live theme —
  worth confirming independently of SEO.
- **Fixed so far:** ~10 MB of retired images deferred (see T4). The GLB and video need a design
  decision (compression, or a poster-first strategy), not an SEO edit.

### P6 — GA4 / conversion tracking not visible
- **Severity:** Medium · **Fixed:** ❌ needs admin access
- **Evidence:** the live home page contains no GTM container, no GA4 measurement ID and no
  `gtag('config'` call. Only Shopify's own `trekkie` analytics is present. The Google Search
  Console verification meta tag **is** present and was preserved.
- **Important caveat:** Shopify's newer Customer Events / Web Pixels load in a sandboxed worker
  and may not appear in the page HTML. So this is **not** proof GA4 is missing — it needs
  confirming in admin (Settings → Customer events). I'm flagging it rather than asserting it.

### T2 — Redirect chain on the apex domain
`http://saialondon.com/` → `https://saialondon.com/` → `https://www.saialondon.com/`. Two hops
where one would do. Shopify manages this and doesn't expose the rule; the cost is a few hundred
milliseconds for the small share of traffic arriving on bare `http://` + apex. Not worth chasing.

### T4 — 10 MB of images that never display
- **Affected:** `index.html:677-707`, `theme/templates/index.liquid`
- **Evidence:** 15 `figure-*.png` (~500 KB each) plus `mat-stage-baked.png`. Both layers are
  hidden on **every** path — `js/home-journey.js:321-323` sets `display:none` on the WebGL path,
  and `index.html:59-60` hides them via `.is-static` on the mobile/reduced-motion path. A stale
  comment in `home-journey.js:322` claims the static poster still uses the first frame; the CSS
  disproves it. `display:none` does not prevent an image from being fetched.
- **Fix applied:** `loading="lazy" decoding="async"` on all 16. A lazy image inside a
  `display:none` container never intersects the viewport, so it is never fetched — and if any
  future path reveals the layer, the images load then. Nothing was deleted, so every code path
  still works.
- **Measured:** headless load before/after — figure requests dropped from 15 to 0.
  **9.96 MB removed from the home page's initial load.**
- **Residual:** `mat-stage-baked.png` (0.8 MB) still loads. It sits at `opacity:0` rather than
  `display:none`, so it does intersect the viewport and lazy-loading can't defer it. It is dead on
  both paths, so it can be removed — but that's a markup change to a live animation, so I've left
  it and flagged it rather than risking the scroll flow for 0.8 MB.
- **Risk:** low. No markup removed, no styles changed, smoke-tested with zero console errors.

## 8. Content and internal-linking findings

**Nothing was deleted in the redesign.** Every legacy page, article and product still exists at its
original URL with its original content. The regression was in *linking* and *labelling*, which is
why it's recoverable.

| Surface | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Blog (28 articles) | 0 internal links | linked from every page footer |
| ~29 legacy pages | no nav, no footer | full site chrome |
| `/collections/all` | dead end | 3 outbound links |
| `/products/*` | title + price only | description + 2 outbound links |

**Anchor text** is descriptive throughout — "Hire mats for your event", "Pilates with Cristina",
"Our story". No "click here" or "learn more" anywhere in the site's navigation or footers.

**Crawlable navigation:** all primary navigation uses real `<a href>` elements, not JS click
handlers. The mobile drawer is real anchors too. No crawl-path problems there.

**Click depth** is now at most 2 from the home page for every indexable surface, except the ~29
legacy pages, which are reachable via the sitemap and (now) via footer links from any page they
link to — but are still not linked *to* from the main navigation. That is correct: they shouldn't
be in the nav. Once P1 and P2 are resolved in admin, the remaining set will be small.

## 9. Performance and Core Web Vitals findings

Diagnosed from asset weights and load behaviour rather than field data — the theme is one day old,
so CrUX has no data for it yet.

| Metric | Main risk | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **LCP** | 10 MB of hidden images competing for bandwidth; 9.7 MB GLB; render-blocking Google Fonts | ✅ 10 MB removed; fonts and GLB outstanding |
| **CLS** | low — images sit in CSS-sized containers with `object-fit: cover` | acceptable as-is |
| **INP** | scroll-scrubbed WebGL animation is the main cost | not assessed; needs field data |
| **TTFB** | Shopify CDN; home page responded in ~270 ms | ✅ good |

**On `width`/`height` attributes:** theme check flags 86 images for missing dimensions, and my
first read of this was that it meant CLS risk. Looking at the actual CSS, it mostly doesn't: images
render inside `.saia-slot` containers that set `width:100%; height:100%; object-fit:cover`
(`css/base.css:110`), so the container defines the box and the image can't reflow it. Adding
attributes site-wide would also need a global `img { height: auto }` rule first, or CSS-constrained
images would distort. I've deliberately **not** made that change — it is a real risk of visible
distortion for a benefit this layout has largely already secured. Worth doing properly as its own
task with visual checks.

**What I did do:** added `fetchpriority="high"` to the single above-the-fold hero on each editorial
page (they were already `loading="eager"`, so this only changes network priority, never layout).

**Render-blocking fonts (Medium, not fixed):** every page loads Google Fonts via a blocking
`<link rel="stylesheet">`. `&display=swap` is set, so text stays visible, but the request still
blocks first paint. Self-hosting the two families in `theme/assets/` would remove a third-party
round trip on the critical path. Worth doing; needs a visual check that the fallback stack matches.

## 11. Recommended improvements (not implemented)

Ordered by value.

1. **Confirm GA4 is connected** (admin). Without measurement, everything else is guesswork.
2. **Resolve the founder-page cannibalisation** (P2) — consolidate `/pages/my-story` and
   `/pages/about` into `/pages/story`, guided by Search Console impressions.
3. **Redirect the thin legacy pages** (P1) — seven URLs, each to its closest equivalent.
4. **Move the mobile flow copy into HTML** (P3) — the biggest remaining on-page content risk,
   but it needs its own task because it touches an animation the client values.
5. **Self-host the fonts** — removes a render-blocking third-party request on every page.
6. **Compress or defer the 9.7 MB GLB and fix the `flow.mp4` 404** — the largest remaining LCP
   lever, and the 404 may indicate a broken render path worth investigating on its own merits.
7. **Add `<h2>` structure to `story.html`** — visual output unchanged, semantics improved.
8. **Consider FAQPage schema on `/pages/faq`** — only once that page's content is reviewed; the
   markup must match visible Q&A exactly, and I haven't verified its current content.

Deliberately **not** recommended: Product/Offer schema (mats are hire-only — "buy" rich results
would contradict the brand rule and mislead), Review or AggregateRating schema (no review content
exists — fabricating it is out of the question), Event schema for the five past events (low value,
and the dates would need verifying), and LocalBusiness with a street address (the NW3 base isn't
published; the Northern Ireland registered office in the terms page is not a service location).

## Verdict

**Yes — the redesign is likely to have hurt SEO, but the damage is shallow and mostly already
reversed.**

The distinction that matters: the migration did not break the site's *structure*. URLs were
preserved, handles match, HTTPS and `www` are enforced, the sitemap is intact, nothing 404s, and no
content was deleted. Those are the migration failures that are genuinely hard to recover from, and
none of them happened here.

What did break was the layer on top — the labels and the links. The home page told Google it was a
social club rather than a mat-hire business, 28 articles and 29 pages were left with no way in, and
two extra copies of the site were competing with the original. Those cost visibility, and on the
home page the cost was real, because that page carries the commercial intent for the whole
business. But they cost visibility in a way that titles, links and redirects fix directly, and
recovery should follow within a few weeks of the theme push.

The timing helps considerably. The theme went live on 2026-07-22 and this audit ran on 2026-07-23 —
one day. Search engines had barely begun to recrawl. Fixing this now, before the old signals decay
and before Google settles on a canonical among the three hosts, is worth far more than the same
fixes would be in a month.

Two honest caveats. First, I have no Search Console or analytics access, so I cannot show measured
traffic loss — every finding here is diagnosed from served HTML and URL inventories, and I've kept
"confirmed regression" strictly for things verified against the live site. Second, the theme
changes are staged in the repo and unverified against a real Shopify render; `theme check` reports
zero syntax errors and the new snippet uses the same `{% render %}`-from-layout pattern as
`saia-boot.liquid` (which works in production today), but the draft-theme preview in §14 is the
step that actually proves it. Please run that before publishing.
